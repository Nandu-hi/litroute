M6 PLAN - RELEVANCE SCORER
LitRoute (CS5013), package com.litroute.scoring
Updated 2026-10-14 to match the code as built.


1. PURPOSE
----------
M6 decides which student each new paper is for. It takes the deduplicated papers of a batch
(M4's output) and the students' profiles (M5's output). It assigns every paper to the one
student it suits best, and recommends each student's best 2-3 papers to the professor. This
follows the professor's requirement in the proposal: "we need 2-3 papers personalised
individually to multiple students".


2. REQUIREMENTS
---------------
R1  Each paper goes to exactly one student: the one it is most relevant to. No paper is routed
    to several students.
R2  Of each student's papers in a batch, the best 2-3 are recommended (default 3).
R3  No paper is hidden. Every paper is returned and queued for review (M7e). One that is not
    recommended carries a note saying why.
R4  Only research papers are recommended. Book reviews, discussions and closures, corrections
    and errata, retractions, editorials, obituaries, letters and comments are queued but never
    recommended.
R5  A paper that does not look like any student's work is not recommended, even if it is the
    best a student got in that batch.
R6  Every score comes with a readable rationale the professor can check in M8.
R7  Acceptance criterion from the proposal: the papers recommended to each student are at
    least 80% theirs (per-student precision of the top 3, P@3 >= 0.8).


3. INPUTS AND OUTPUTS
---------------------
Input   Every student's Profile from M7d: reference papers with SPECTER2 vectors and text
        (see m5plan.txt). Profiles must be built with the same model as the provider.
Input   EmbeddingProvider: SPECTER2, to embed candidates exactly as the profiles were embedded.
Input   The batch: deduplicated PaperCandidates from M7c, or local PDFs (LocalPdfCandidates).
Output  Exactly one ScoredPaper per candidate, in batch order: the assigned student, a score
        from 0 to 1, the rationale, status PENDING and a recommended flag. The CLI adds them to
        the M7e review queue.


4. SCORING METHOD
-----------------
4.1 Evidence
    A candidate is scored from the best evidence it has:
      FULL_TEXT   its PDF, if M2 saved a readable one. It is extracted and embedded exactly like
                  a reference paper (M5's PdfTextExtractor and PaperEmbedder), using the
                  candidate's own title, which is more reliable than one read off the PDF.
      ABSTRACT    otherwise its title and abstract
      TITLE       otherwise its title alone; scores from a title alone are less certain
    Comparisons are like for like: a full PDF against the reference papers' combined vectors;
    an abstract or title against their abstract views. The evidence read for a candidate is
    cached, so scoring it again (by another strategy, say) does not read or embed it twice.

4.2 Meaning signal (SPECTER2)
    The candidate's similarity to a student is
        0.5 x cosine to the student's centroid (the normalised mean of all their papers)
      + 0.5 x mean cosine to the student's 3 closest papers.
    The centroid captures the student's work as a whole. The closest papers let a candidate
    that matches only part of it still score well. Profiles are uniform: there are no
    interest clusters (see section 6).

4.3 Wording signal (TF-IDF)
    LexicalIndex builds TF-IDF vectors over every reference paper's title, abstract and body.
    Terms are stemmed words and pairs of adjacent words ("rank aggregation"), after dropping
    words shorter than 3 letters, English function words and boilerplate such as figure, table
    and results. A dropped word separates its neighbours, so no pair spans it. Terms used in only
    one paper (often extraction noise) or in more than half of all papers carry no information
    about who a paper belongs to, and are left out.
        idf = ln((N + 1) / (df + 1)) + 1,   tf weight = 1 + ln(count),   vectors unit length
    A candidate's wording similarity to a student is blended the same way as meaning: half its
    similarity to the student's papers taken together, half the mean of the 3 closest papers.

4.4 Fit and share: how much of the paper belongs to each student
    Each signal is turned into z-scores across the students, and the two are averaged into the
    paper's fit with each student. This means a paper close to every student, such as civil
    engineering in general, stands out for nobody. The share is softmax(2 x fit): the fraction
    of the paper's match that goes to each student. A sharpness of 2 gives a clear match most of
    the share (0.6-0.85) without hiding the runner-up.

4.5 Relevance: how much the paper looks like the student's own work
    Relevance is absolute, not relative to the other students. For each signal and each kind of
    evidence, two anchors are measured from the profiles themselves. Each reference paper is
    held out in turn and compared, as a candidate would be, with what remains. The median
    similarity to its own student is the "own" anchor; the median to other students is the
    "other" anchor. Each signal's similarity is placed on the scale where "other" is 0 and
    "own" is 1. The two positions are averaged and passed through a logistic curve, giving
    about 0.9 at a typical own paper and about 0.1 at a typical other student's paper. Title
    evidence is calibrated with the reference papers' titles embedded on their own. Fixed
    fallback anchors, measured on the eight reference profiles, are used only when the loaded
    profiles cannot provide their own (a single student, or students with one paper each).

4.6 Score
    score = relevance x share. A paper is assigned to the student with the highest score.

4.7 Duplicates
    Two reference files with the same SHA-256, or vectors at cosine >= 0.99, are the same
    paper saved twice and count once, both when comparing and when calibrating.


5. ASSIGNMENT AND RECOMMENDATION (RelevanceScorer.match)
--------------------------------------------------------
1. Score every candidate for every student, in parallel, and keep its best student (R1).
2. Not recommended, with a note:
     - a note rather than a research paper: recognised from the alert's section label (e.g.
       ASCE's "Book Reviews", "Discussions and Closures", "Corrections") or from the title
       ("Erratum for ...", "Discussion of ...", "Closure to ...") (R4);
     - relevance below 0.50 - the paper looks no more like this student's papers than like
       other students' papers (R5).
3. Of each student's remaining papers, the 3 best-scored are recommended (R2). The rest get
   the note "<student> has 3 better-scored papers in this batch".
4. Return every paper, recommended or not (R3).

Rationale (R6), in this form:
    Best match: <share>% of the paper's match goes to <student> (next: <runner-up> <share>%).
    Relevance <r> (a student's own papers typically 0.9, other students' 0.1).
    Shared words: <up to 5>. Closest in <student>: "<title>" (<similarity>), ... (3 papers)
    Scored from <its full PDF | its title and abstract | its title alone>.
    [Not recommended: <reason>]

Why the floor is a fixed 0.50: a floor set per kind of evidence (the 5th percentile of
students' own held-out papers) was tried first. For title-only evidence it came to 0.25, and
it let through a SpringerOpen paper on ceramic coatings that the professor had marked
irrelevant (relevance 0.29 from its title alone). 0.50 is the midpoint between "like other
students' papers" and "like this student's own".


6. DESIGN DECISIONS, AS MEASURED
--------------------------------
Every choice below was measured on the professor's own data. The test set is
~/.litroute/match-test: 48 new PDFs filed by the professor under the student each should reach
(46 readable, 2 scans). They were scored against the eight full profiles (294 papers).
"Correct" means assigned to the student the paper is filed under.

Current code, by strategy:
                                   correct      P@3
    Full PDF (48 papers)
        SPECTER2 meaning only      43 / 48      0.81
        TF-IDF wording only        43 / 48      0.88
        Both (what M6 uses)        46 / 48      1.00     20 recommended, all 20 correct
    Title + abstract only (46 readable papers, as for a paywalled alert paper)
        SPECTER2 meaning only      42 / 46      0.81
        TF-IDF wording only        44 / 46      1.00
        Both (what M6 uses)        44 / 46      1.00

Decisions
- Combine meaning and wording. The first M6 used SPECTER2 alone and routed papers to several
  students. It placed 42 of 48 at the right student, sent each paper to 2.3 students on
  average, and reached P@3 0.65, below the 0.8 criterion. The eight students all work in
  structural and hazard engineering, so SPECTER2 puts nearly every paper 0.85-0.95 from every
  student. Wording separates neighbouring fields better, and both together do best.
- One student per paper (R1), on the professor's instruction.
- Keyword-only wording (dropping all general English vocabulary) was tried and rejected. It
  was no better than plain TF-IDF and worse on title-only alerts, because general-English word
  lists also contain domain words such as earthquake, fire, flood and risk.
- Fine-tuning SPECTER2 on the students' folders with a triplet loss (the transferable idea of
  the SEMFormer paper, MM '24) was tried and rejected: 82.3% vs 81.6% in 5-fold
  cross-validation, no real gain.
- Interest clustering of profiles was removed on 2026-10-14. With and without it, the results
  were identical: 46/48, P@3 1.00, 20 of 20 recommendations correct. Uniform profiles are
  simpler.
- A fixed relevance floor of 0.50 instead of a per-evidence percentile (section 5).


7. COMPONENTS
-------------
RelevanceScorer     The matcher: evidence, both signals, calibration, scoring, match(). Thread-safe.
                    Strategies: SEMANTIC, LEXICAL, HYBRID (the default); the other two exist for
                    comparison.
LexicalIndex        TF-IDF index over the reference papers; closeness of a text to each student;
                    the shared terms quoted in rationales.
Terms               Text -> countable terms: case and accent folding, stop words and boilerplate,
                    stemming, adjacent-word pairs.
LocalPdfCandidates  Turns a PDF that did not come from an alert into a candidate, to see where it
                    would be routed.
MatchEvaluation     Agreement with the professor's filing: papers assigned correctly, and P@3.
                    A student's P@3 counts at most as many recommendations as papers filed for
                    them, so a student with one filed paper is judged on one.
RelevanceScorerCli  Command-line entry point (section 8).
The constructor rejects profiles built with another model. When scoring uses wording, it also
rejects profiles saved without their text (rebuild them with ProfileBuilderCli first).


8. RUNNING IT
-------------
    mvn -q compile exec:java -Dexec.mainClass=com.litroute.scoring.RelevanceScorerCli [-Dexec.args=--top=2]
        Matches the deduplicated papers in M7c against the M7d profiles, adds the matches to the
        M7e review queue, and lists each student's recommended papers.

    mvn -q compile exec:java -Dexec.mainClass=com.litroute.scoring.RelevanceScorerCli -Dexec.args="PDF_OR_FOLDER [--top=2]"
        Scores each PDF for every student and changes nothing. If the subfolders are named
        after students (FOLDER/RP3-mf-sim/x.pdf), it also compares the three strategies on
        papers assigned correctly and P@3.


9. TEST PLAN
------------
Real data only: the real SPECTER2 model, the students' real profiles, the papers the professor
filed by student, and the client's six real alert emails run through M2-M4 offline from
recorded HTTP replies.

RelevanceScorerTest
  - Every paper is matched to exactly one student.
  - Filed papers reach their student: at least 95% of the readable ones and 90% of all.
  - Title and abstract alone still find the student for at least 90%.
  - Recommendations meet the proposal's target: P@3 >= 0.8, from full text and from title and
    abstract.
  - No student gets more than the requested number of recommendations (3, and 2 when asked).
  - Words and meaning together do at least as well as SPECTER2 alone on both papers assigned
    correctly and P@3, and better on at least one of them.
  - Every score is between 0 and 1, best first, and explained.
  - A student's own reference paper, offered as a candidate, scores best for that student.
  - A blank profile scores zero instead of failing.
  - Profiles from another model are rejected; profiles without text are rejected unless
    scoring by meaning alone.
  - The client's alert papers: each goes to one student; the SpringerOpen paper the professor
    called irrelevant is not recommended, the SSI review the professor called highly relevant
    is, and the ASCE issue's two book reviews, discussion, closure and erratum never are.
LexicalIndexTest    A held-out reference paper is closest to its own student; shared terms name
                    what a paper and a student have in common.
TermsTest           Stop words and boilerplate dropped; hyphenated words kept whole; adjacent
                    pairs formed but not across a dropped word; plurals and accents folded.

Status: all pass on the full profiles (the whole suite, 199 tests).


10. KNOWN WEAKNESSES
--------------------
- The two misses on the full profiles:
    - "Adnan et al (2024) reportD2.2" (filed RP4) is a scan with no text, so only its file name
      could be matched; it went to RP7.
    - "Multi-hazard disaster scenario method and emergency management for urban resilience"
      (filed RP4) went to RP1. It sits between multi-hazard characterisation and multi-hazard
      risk.
- Neighbouring students are where errors happen: RP5 wildfire risk vs RP6 wildfire emissions,
  RP3 multi-fidelity simulation vs RP8 uncertainty quantification, and RP1 vs RP4. With only
  5-7 papers per student these pairs were often mixed up (35 of 48 correct); with the full
  folders only the RP4/RP1 paper above is.
- Title-only alert papers are scored from much less evidence, so their scores are less
  certain; the rationale says so, and they must still reach relevance 0.50 to be recommended.
